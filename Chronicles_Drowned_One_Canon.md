# Chronicles — The Drowned One & The Sixty-Year Mystery

## CANON — LOCKED

This is the development reference for the established truth behind Greyhaven's lighthouse mystery and the Drowned One. The player should discover this information gradually rather than receiving it as a single explanation.

### The Drowned One
The Drowned One is an ancient sea horror dwelling in the deep water beyond Greyhaven. It is drawn toward the lighthouse beacon. The light acts as a signal that the Drowned One can perceive, causing it to approach Greyhaven.

The creature has not properly attacked Greyhaven for approximately sixty years because the lighthouse has remained dark.

### What Happened Sixty Years Ago
Sixty years before the events of Chronicles, Greyhaven suffered a catastrophic storm while its lighthouse was operating.

During the storm, lightning struck the lighthouse. The beacon illuminated. The Drowned One perceived the light and came toward it.

The resulting attack devastated the harbour and caused many deaths. The lighthouse Keeper died during the disaster.

His spirit, however, remained bound to the lighthouse.

### The Keeper's Spirit
The Keeper is dead. Only his spirit remains within the lighthouse during normal, non-stormy conditions.

After his death, the Keeper learned or understood the terrible truth: the lighthouse beacon attracts the Drowned One.

He therefore took on a single purpose:

**Keep the lighthouse dark.**

The Keeper is not malicious. He believes keeping the beacon dark is the only way to protect Greyhaven.

### Lightning and the Lighthouse
During severe storms, lightning can strike the lighthouse.

The supernatural force of the lightning temporarily disrupts the Keeper's ability to remain in the physical plane. The Keeper disappears and the lighthouse beacon illuminates.

This explains the occasional reports from Greyhaven townsfolk that the lighthouse has mysteriously lit during storms.

These storm-caused illuminations do not last long enough for the Drowned One to fully reach Greyhaven. When the storm passes and the beacon goes dark, the Drowned One retreats and the Keeper's spirit can return.

Therefore, the Drowned One has not properly attacked Greyhaven during the sixty years since the original disaster.

### The Critical Difference: The Player's Beacon
A natural lightning strike causes only a temporary illumination.

The player lighting the lighthouse deliberately is different.

If the player restores and lights the beacon, the light remains active. The sustained beacon provides the Drowned One with a continuous signal.

The Drowned One therefore comes toward Greyhaven.

The player does not understand this when choosing to restore the lighthouse.

**The player believes they are helping Greyhaven.**

They are not knowingly summoning the BBEG.

### The Keeper Encounter
When the player reaches the lighthouse, they encounter the Keeper as a spirit.

He attempts to convince them that the lighthouse must remain dark. His explanation should sound suspicious enough that the player can reasonably doubt him.

The player can choose to believe him.

If they believe him:
- The Keeper provides a fuller explanation.
- There is no Keeper combat.
- The player learns substantially more about what happened sixty years ago.

If they refuse to believe him and attempt to light the beacon:
- The Keeper opposes them.
- A Keeper combat encounter can occur.
- Following the Keeper's defeat, the player can choose whether to light the beacon.

Neither route should immediately reveal every aspect of the Drowned One's nature.

### Greyhaven's Two Believable Opinions

**Those who want the lighthouse lit** believe the dark lighthouse is damaging Greyhaven. Ships need a functioning coastal beacon, trade has declined, the harbour has suffered, and a working lighthouse could make ships more willing to visit.

**Those who believe it should remain dark** have inherited fragments of the old story. They may believe the lighthouse was darkened for a reason, something terrible happened the last time it operated, or that some things are better left forgotten.

Neither side should know the complete truth.

### Bellwatch Church
Bellwatch Church contains surviving historical records concerning the old harbour, lighthouse and disaster.

The records should NOT simply reveal that the lighthouse summons the Drowned One.

Instead, the player should uncover fragments, inconsistencies and contradictions, including possibilities such as:
- the official account of the catastrophic storm;
- the lighthouse being struck and damaged;
- the Keeper's death;
- the lighthouse remaining unlit afterward;
- evidence that keeping the beacon dark was deliberate;
- records of later unexplained lighthouse illuminations during storms;
- hints that the original Keeper or witnesses knew more than the official account admits;
- damaged or missing passages referring to something in the water.

The player should leave Bellwatch understanding that the accepted history of the lighthouse is incomplete, while still lacking the full truth.

### Narrative Principle
The central irony is:

**Greyhaven genuinely needs the lighthouse.**

The player has good reason to believe restoring it will help the town.

The Keeper genuinely believes keeping it dark is necessary to protect the town.

Both sides have understandable motivations.

The tragedy is that the player can make the apparently heroic choice to restore the beacon and unknowingly bring the Drowned One to Greyhaven.

### Final Revelation
Once the beacon is deliberately lit and remains active:
1. The lighthouse continues to shine.
2. The sea begins to change.
3. The atmosphere becomes unnaturally quiet.
4. The sea falls silent.
5. The Drowned One responds to the sustained beacon.
6. The creature emerges from the ocean.
7. The BBEG encounter begins.

The reveal should echo the creature's defining characteristic:

**The first thing the player notices is the silence.**

### Development Rules
- Do not reveal the complete truth too early.
- Bellwatch provides fragments, not exposition dumps.
- Townsfolk provide conflicting but believable opinions.
- The Keeper's warnings must be credible but suspicious.
- The player must be allowed to believe or reject the Keeper.
- Essential story information should have sensible alternative discovery routes.
- Pip remembers meaningful discoveries, knowledge, relationships and consequences, not every action.
- Action Checks are introduced naturally when the story creates meaningful uncertainty.
- Preserve existing working systems and avoid unnecessary rewrites.
