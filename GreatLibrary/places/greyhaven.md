[TITLE]

Greyhaven

The Last Safe Harbour Before the Black Sea

[SUMMARY]

Greyhaven is a weather-beaten port town perched upon the western cliffs, standing as the last safe harbour before the endless waters of the Grey Expanse. Shrouded in mist and shaped by centuries of storms, it is a place where every cobbled street, weathered dock, and creaking building holds a story.

Life revolves around the sea. Fishing, trade, and shipbuilding sustain the town, while ancient traditions and superstitions guide its people. They believe the sea remembers, the church bell warns of coming tragedy, and some mysteries are best left undisturbed.

Though welcoming to weary travellers, the people of Greyhaven are guarded, speaking little of the old legends or the strange events that still haunt the harbour. Beneath its humble appearance lies a deeper purpose: Greyhaven is not merely a town, but an ancient watchpost, standing vigil over the dark waters beyond and whatever may one day return from them.


[OVERVIEW]

Greyhaven is a weather-beaten port town perched upon the jagged western coast, where the cliffs descend into a restless sea known simply as The Grey Expanse. It is the final settlement before civilization gives way to endless water and forgotten horizons.

For centuries, sailors have called it the town that listens.

The people who live here would tell you something different.

"It remembers."

Greyhaven has survived storms that erased kingdoms, famed pirate fleets, famines, plague, and wars that never reached its walls because the sea swallowed the armies first.

Nothing here is pristine.

Everything has a story.

Every cracked cobblestone, warped timber, rusted anchor and weathered bell carries a history someone refuses to forget.

Visitors often describe the town as strangely quiet.

Not silent.

Just...

Respectfully quiet.


[FOUNDING]

No one truly knows who built Greyhaven.

The official records claim fishermen settled the bay nearly eight hundred years ago after discovering unusually calm waters behind the cliffs.

Older stories disagree.

According to local legend, there was already a harbour waiting.

Stone piers.

Mooring posts.

A lighthouse.

Everything.

No builders.

No inhabitants.

Just empty docks.

The first settlers simply moved in.

Some believe another civilization vanished overnight.

Others insist Greyhaven was abandoned because whatever once lived beneath the sea began to wake.

The oldest maps simply label the bay:

"Safe... for now."


[GEOGRAPHY]

Greyhaven is built around a deep natural harbour protected by towering black cliffs.

Its districts climb the hillside in uneven terraces connected by steep stone stairways.

Major landmarks include:

The Old Harbour

Fisherman's Row

Bellwatch Church

Lantern Square

The Salt Market

Widow's Bluff

The Breakwater

The Old Lighthouse

Black Gull Docks


Everything smells faintly of salt, smoke and damp rope.

Even inland homes carry the scent.


[CLIMATE]

Greyhaven rarely enjoys clear weather.

Most days are wrapped in mist.

Rain comes often.

Storms come without warning.

Locals judge the coming weather not by clouds...

...but by silence.

If the gulls stop calling...

Boats remain tied.

Nobody asks why.


[ARCHITECTURE]

Greyhaven was never designed.

It simply grew.

Buildings lean against one another as though supporting old friends.

Roofs are repaired with mismatched slate.

Windows rarely match.

Every doorway carries charms against bad luck.

Shells.

Knotted rope.

Small carved whales.

Coins driven into beams.

No two homes are identical.

Generations simply built onto what already existed.


[HARBOUR]

The harbour is Greyhaven's beating heart.

Fishing vessels arrive before dawn.

Merchant ships dock weekly.

Whalers appear only in winter.

Occasionally...

Ships arrive with no crew.

The harbour master records these as:

"Returned."

Never:

"Found."

The distinction matters.

No one explains why.


[ECONOMY]

Greyhaven survives through:

Fishing

Salt production

Ship repair

Timber imports

Whale oil

Trade caravans

Salvage operations


Salvage is surprisingly profitable.

Storms frequently destroy ships upon the surrounding reefs.

By ancient agreement...

Anything claimed by the sea belongs to the sea.

Anything washed ashore belongs to Greyhaven.


[GOVERNMENT]

Greyhaven is governed by the Harbour Council.

Seven members oversee the town.

They include:

Harbour Master

Head Priest

Market Steward

Captain Representative

Guild Foreman

Town Magistrate

Keeper of Records


The mayor technically exists.

Nobody remembers the last decision they made.

The Harbour Council quietly handles everything.


[RELIGION]

The dominant faith centres around The Keeper of Tides, protector of sailors and guardian of those lost at sea.

Every voyage begins with a blessing.

Every death receives a lantern.

Children learn young:

Never whistle aboard ship.

Never count waves aloud.

Never mock the sea.

It remembers.


[BELLWATCH CHURCH]

The oldest building in Greyhaven.

Its stone predates the town itself.

The church bell is said to ring on its own.

When it does...

Someone dies before sunrise.

No one questions this anymore.

The priest keeps careful records.

Not of who dies.

But of every unexplained ringing.

Some pages have been torn out.


[THE OLD LIGHTHOUSE]

The lighthouse no longer guides ships.

Its light has been extinguished for nearly sixty years.

Yet sailors still claim to see it burning during violent storms.

Especially...

Ships that never reach harbour.

No keeper lives there.

No one has for decades.

Yet every morning...

Fresh footprints sometimes appear on the spiral staircase.


[FISHERMAN'S ROW]

The oldest inhabited street.

Every family living there has worked the sea for generations.

Doors remain unlocked.

Not because crime is rare.

Because neighbours enter freely whenever storms arrive.

If someone's lantern isn't lit by nightfall...

People check.

No invitation required.


[THE SALT MARKET]

The busiest square in town.

Fish.

Timber.

Cloth.

Imported spices.

Navigation charts.

Stories.

Stories are bought here almost as often as goods.

Travelling sailors can earn free meals simply by telling a tale no one has heard before.

Lies are surprisingly common.

Good lies become local legends.


[THE BENT ANCHOR TAVERN]

The unofficial heart of Greyhaven.

Captains negotiate contracts here.

Fishermen complain here.

Musicians perform here.

Rumours begin here.

If something strange happens in town...

The Bent Anchor hears it first.

The owner claims every table has witnessed history.

He's probably right.


[WIDOW'S BLUFF]

A windswept overlook above the harbour.

Originally named after generations of women who waited there for ships that never returned.

Today...

Lanterns are still lit there every autumn.

Some swear distant lights answer from the sea.


[THE BLACK GULL DOCKS]

Used primarily by long-distance merchants.

Few locals linger after dark.

The warehouses creak constantly.

Even without wind.

Dockworkers insist that's just old timber.

Nobody believes them.


[LAWS OF GREYHAVEN]

Visitors find several laws unusual.

No bells may ring after sunset except church bells.

No ship may depart during unnatural fog.

No one may remove driftwood from Widow's Bluff.

Any unidentified corpse found ashore must remain untouched until dawn.

Children are forbidden from playing near abandoned boats.

Breaking these laws rarely results in prison.

People simply...

Stop speaking to you.


[FESTIVALS]

Festival of Returning Lights

Held each autumn.

Thousands of floating lanterns are released into the harbour to honour those lost at sea.

Sometimes...

One returns.

Still burning.

Nobody retrieves it.


[STORM EVE]

Celebrated before winter.

Families remain indoors.

Stories are shared.

Doors remain bolted.

Candles stay lit until sunrise.

Visitors often ask what they're hiding from.

Locals smile politely.

"The weather."


[LOCAL SUPERSTITIONS]

Greyhaven has hundreds.

The most common include:

Never sleep facing the sea.

Never answer a voice calling your name from the fog.

Never sail on perfectly calm water.

If a gull lands beside you three times...

Leave town.

If your reflection smiles first...

Find a priest.

If the tide retreats too quickly...

Run uphill.


[FAMOUS FIGURES]

Captain Aldren Vale

Legendary explorer who vanished pursuing an impossible horizon.

His ship supposedly returns once every generation.

Always empty.


Sister Miriam 

Priestess of Bellwatch.

Kept meticulous journals spanning forty years.

The final volume ends mid-sentence.

No body was ever found.


Dockmaster Harrow

Served sixty-three years.

Claimed he recognised every ship by its footsteps.

No one understood what he meant.


[CREATURES OF THE BAY]

Most are harmless.

Harbour seals.

Grey dolphins.

White gulls.

Then there are the others.

Long shadows beneath still water.

Fish that refuse to surface.

Whales that sing only beneath full moons.

Nobody names them.

Naming attracts attention.


[THE SEA BEYOND]

West of Greyhaven lies the Grey Expanse.

Maps become unreliable after several weeks' sailing.

Compasses drift.

Stars appear unfamiliar.

Entire islands seem to move.

Some captains claim there are places where dawn never arrives.

Others insist the ocean itself dreams.

Few journeys venture that far.

Fewer return.


[THE PEOPLE OF GREYHAVEN]

Greyhaven folk are practical, stubborn, and slow to trust.

They speak plainly.

Work hard.

Celebrate modestly.

Grieve together.

Hospitality is sacred, but so is caution.

If a traveller arrives soaked from a storm, they will be offered dry clothes, hot stew, and a bed without question.

If that same traveller begins asking about certain old stories, abandoned ships, or the bell that rings by itself, conversations have a habit of ending abruptly.

Not from fear alone.

From experience.

The people of Greyhaven believe that some questions are like casting a hook into dark water.

You never know what might bite.


[GREATEST SECRET]

The oldest families keep one truth from outsiders.

Greyhaven was never built because this was a good harbour.

It was built because this was where something beneath the sea first stirred.

Every lighthouse, every church bell, every festival, every superstition, every weathered law has the same hidden purpose.

Not to honour the sea.

Not to appease it.

To watch it.

To remember.

And to make certain that, if the Black Sails should ever return from the horizon, there will still be someone waiting on the docks who knows exactly what they mean.
